# Comparison matrix (draft)

*Desk research, 23 Nov 2026. No interviews or usability tests yet. A score appears **only where we have a source**; "—" means not assessed, not "bad". Sources are linked in each row and listed in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).*

## Criteria and scale

The five criteria come from [RESEARCH_PLAN.md](../RESEARCH_PLAN.md).

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Findability** | No current page or document found | Found, but only as a PDF, on a third-party site, or outside the main navigation | A current page in the main navigation of the official site |
| **Clarity** | Rules contradict each other or changed without one current statement | One current statement, but with gaps or vague wording | One current, dated statement with concrete numbers (deadline, fee, phone) |
| **Language accessibility** | Only the local language | English | English plus a language the newcomer reads natively (Russian for Kyrgyz students, Korean for Korean students) |
| **Completeness** | Covers none of the first-week tasks we list | Covers some (for example, arrival and housing only) | Covers arrival, registration, insurance, money, phone and emergency |
| **Actionability** | No steps, or steps that cannot be done by a newcomer | Steps exist, but depend on something the newcomer does not have yet | Steps a newcomer can follow on day one, with a channel to ask |

## What the draft shows

1. **The barrier is different in each country.** In Korea the first-week loop is digital identity: ARC → own-name phone number → bank and app sign-ups. In Kyrgyzstan the weak points are changing registration rules and the lack of a multilingual help line.
2. **Kyrgyz is absent from every official Korean channel we checked;** Russian is the working language for Kyrgyz students in Korea.
3. **University orientation guides are hard to find on both sides.** Only Sogang and Inha have identifiable English guide documents.

## Limits of this draft

- Scores are the team's judgement against the scale above, from desk research only. They are not user-tested.
- Section A is a search-and-browse check; a page-by-page audit (and a click count from the home page) is still to do.
- National-level rows rely partly on media that quote ministries, not on primary texts.
